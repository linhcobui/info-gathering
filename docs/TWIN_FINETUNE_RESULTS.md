# TWIN-FINETUNE — results

Auto-generated by `scripts/twin_ft_report.py` (re-run: `pixi run twin-ft-report`). Plan: [TWIN_FINETUNE.md](TWIN_FINETUNE.md).

**Set-up.** Stage 1 (unaligned baseline, no-obs decoder) trained on **0–3 swaps** (`data/memtest4_s3.zarr`, 378 episodes). Its readout + pixel decoder are frozen. The finetune pool (`data/memtest4_ft.zarr`, 598 episodes, 0–4 swaps, fresh seed) is used **without labels**: the only training signal is the frozen decoder's imagined lift against the real frames' pixels. It contains 4-swap episodes from only **half** of the 243 possible 4-swap sequences; the other half is held out. Test = `memtest4_eval` (0–4, 315 episodes) + `memtest4_eval5` (5 swaps, 63), untouched.

## Reading (written 2026-10-05, after all five runs finished)

**Short version: the label-free finetune fixes the sequences it practised on, and nothing else.**

1. **4-swap sequences that were in the finetune pool: real gain.** Imagined-lift accuracy goes from
   0.58 (C0) to 0.85 (F1, F3). Of these 26 episodes, finetuning fixed 7 that C0 got wrong and broke
   none (p = 0.016).
2. **4-swap sequences held out of the pool: no gain at all.** Every finetune arm fixes 3 and breaks
   3 (p = 1.0). All of them, C0 included, sit at or below the 0.65 you get by always saying
   "empty".
3. **5 swaps: the accuracy rise is a bias, not tracking.** Accuracy goes from 0.65 to 0.68–0.75,
   but only because the finetuned arms say "ball" far less often (16–22% of episodes, against a
   true 35%). Balanced accuracy does not improve (0.61–0.66 vs C0's 0.67), and no difference from
   C0 is significant (p ≥ 0.29).

That meets the plan's second kill criterion: **it improves the pool but not unseen sequences, so
it is memorising.**

**Where the gain on practised sequences comes from.** C0's posterior already held most of the
answer. The frozen reward head, reading C0's belief at the lift onset, was right on 0.81 of those
26 episodes, while C0's imagined frames were right on only 0.58. After finetuning, the head reads
0.85 (one more episode). So the finetune mostly taught the *imagination* (prior plus rendering) to
show what the belief already held. It did not teach the belief to compose new swap orders.

**No decoder-fooling.** In every run the frozen decoder's call and the frozen reward head's call
agree on 90–97% of episodes, and the two accuracies move together. The states did not leave the
region the dictionary understands.

**The recon and twin terms did their jobs in training but changed nothing on test.**

- On the finetune pool, the readout error drops from 0.040 to 0.0005 with the recon term. Without
  it (F1) it rises to 0.063.
- The twin gap stays at 0.0001 with the twin term, against 0.003 without it.
- On test, F1 ≈ F2 ≈ F3, and every EMA twin matches its online copy.

**C1 is not a usable "just more data" control.** It is worse than C0 even on 3-swap sequences it
trained on (0.74 vs 0.93, p = 0.012). It saw 2.6× more episodes in the same 20,000 steps and
finished less well fit: train reward loss 0.098 against C0's 0.038, one-step updater loss 0.00096
against 0.00063. So "F beats C1" says nothing about data. A fair C1 needs about 52k steps, the
same passes per episode as C0.

**What it means.** The reveal reaches the updater, and the updater learns each sequence it is shown,
exactly as stage 1 does. Nothing in this objective rewards an answer that *transfers* to an
ordering it hasn't seen. The failure is the same per-history pattern as before. Feeding more reveal
signal into the same updater doesn't create composition. Only ALIGN-LIFT's labels have done that
so far, by forcing different histories with the same belief onto the same state.
